# Design — Sol Vé Custom Events

The locked design system for this site. Read this file before changing any page.
Extend or amend it when the system needs to grow; do not invent a parallel system in
a stylesheet.

Tokens live in `tokens.css`. Components live in `css/styles.css`. Copy lives in
`content.py`. Pages are generated by `build_site.py`. Nothing is hand-edited in the
`index.html` files — they are build output.

---

## The idea — "The House Volume"

The site is a bound volume. Ink grounds open each chapter and punctuate; paper grounds
carry all sustained reading, the way a printed book alternates plate and text.

The ornament vocabulary is **engraving and fine printing** — the register of a
share certificate, a maison book, a letterpress programme. That vocabulary was chosen
because it does two jobs at once: it is the most opulent thing on the shelf *and* the
most reassuring thing you can hand someone who is risk-averse. The brief was old-world
charm that a corporate buyer still trusts. Engraving is the answer to both halves.

### The brief this replaced

The previous build was a flat near-black agency template set entirely in one geometric
sans, with thumbnail-grade photography upscaled into full-bleed heroes. Thirty people
were shown it; the words used were *bland*, *elementary school*, *discount*, and
*did you pay for this?* Every decision below traces back to one of those.

## Grounds

Two, and only two. A section declares which it sits on and every component inside it
reads the right foreground automatically.

| Class | Ground | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `.on-ink` | `--ink` `#14120C` | chapter plates, punctuation bands, the contact rail, the footer |
| `.on-paper` | `--paper` `#F6F3EC` | every section with more than two paragraphs in it |

Warm near-black derived from the mandated olive charcoal, not `#000` — a true black is
what made the old build feel like a template.

**Never set `--fg` without also setting `--bg`.** Components paint with both (the
primary button's label is `var(--bg)`); half a switch produces ivory-on-ivory. This
already shipped once as an invisible hero button.

Measured contrast, lowest first: gold on paper 5.73:1, gold on ink 8.06:1, muted on
paper 7.27:1, body on paper 16.03:1, body on ink 16.90:1. All AA or better.

## Typography — three faces, three jobs

- **Bodoni Moda** (variable, `opsz` axis) — display only. A true Didone; the hairlines
  are the point, which is why it is only ever used at size with
  `font-optical-sizing: auto`. Never for body copy.
- **Cormorant Garamond** italic — pull-quotes, the hero sub, and `p.beat` (the short
  declarative lines that are the client's own writing voice). The charm register.
  Never a heading.
- **Inter** — body, nav, forms, and the tracked uppercase label register. The
  corporate half of the pairing; it is what keeps the volume legible rather than fussy.

All three are OFL, self-hosted from `assets/fonts/` (latin + latin-ext, 341 KB total).
Not loaded from Google: the wordmark is the first thing anyone sees and it must not
wait on a third-party round-trip.

Body copy is 17px at full contrast. The old build ran 14px at 62% opacity and read as
faint, which is part of what "bland" meant.

## Ornament — rationed

| Device | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Guilloché rosette** (`assets/guilloche.svg`) | behind the hero crest and each chapter plate | one per section, 5–11% opacity, always `aria-hidden` |
| **Engine-turned band** (`assets/guilloche-band.svg`) | the footer crown | once per page |
| **Engraved rule** (`.rule`) | section heads, card divisions | hairline + lozenge node; one per section, maximum |
| **Wax medallion** (`.medallion`) | reserved plates, testimonials | twice per page, maximum |
| **Paper grain** | fixed over the whole page at 2.8% | the ground must read as stock, not as a hex value |

Both guilloché files are generated, not drawn by hand — rotated ellipses about a
centre, which is how an engine-turning lathe actually works. Regenerate rather than
edit. They are applied as CSS `mask` with `background: currentColor`, so they take the
foreground of whatever ground they sit on.

## Macrostructure

Every page opens with a **chapter plate**: folio (`Chapter IV · What We Create`),
engraved rule, display title, lead. The repetition is deliberate — it is what makes
nine separate pages read as one volume.

- Home: full-height crest hero over film, then alternating ink/paper chapters.
- Content pages: chapter plate → paper essay → ink punctuation → paper.
- Division pages: chapter plate → full-bleed plate figure → split → notes → FAQ.
- Every page ends with the **contact rail**, then the footer.

Running text aligns to the same left gutter as its chapter title. Do not centre a
measure under a left-aligned plate; it reads as a mistake.

## Chrome

- **Masthead**: menu left, crest centred, contact button right. Symmetric and formal.
  It always floats over an ink ground, so it hard-sets the ink foreground.
- The crest is hidden on the homepage (`.is-home`) because the hero carries it at full
  size 200px below; showing both reads as an error.
- **Compact bar** appears on scroll: mark, menu, contact.
- **Menu overlay**: full-screen ink, inset gold frame, roman-numbered chapter links
  staggered in, divisions aside, contact block, socials.
- **A contact button is in the masthead of every page at every width**, plus the
  contact rail and the footer. This is a client requirement and `check_site.py`
  rule 7 enforces it.

## Motion

One signature, then restraint.

- **The unveiling** — plates enter behind a curtain that lifts (`clip-path` inset,
  900ms). Not a fade.
- Display lines rise out of their own mask, 60ms stagger.
- Lenis damps the scroll. It is the only motion dependency and is never initialised
  under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Two hard rules learned the hard way:**

1. Every animation whose *starting state hides content* is scoped to `.js` (set by an
   inline script in `<head>`) and has a 1200ms failsafe that reveals anything still
   hidden. A missed animation is a non-event; a permanently blank page is not.
2. `overflow-x: clip` goes on `body`, never on `html`. On the root it makes the
   document a scroll container — a `visible`/`clip` axis pair computes the visible
   axis to `auto` — which silently breaks IntersectionObserver's viewport root and
   left every image on the site clipped and invisible.

## Imagery

Professional photography only. No illustration, no cartoons, no generated imagery, no
stock — the previous build hot-linked six Unsplash photographs as division heroes and
it was one of the reasons the site read as a discount operation.

`assets/photos/manifest.json` is the single source of truth. A photograph renders only
when it is `approved: true` **and** names a photographer. Everything else renders as a
**reserved plate** — medallion, guilloché, label — which is composed on purpose so an
absent image reads as restraint rather than as a hole.

Minimum 2000px on the long edge. The old homepage hero was 442×650 stretched across
1440px and the softness was visible before a word was read.

Import with `tools/import_photos.py`; it refuses undersized files and writes
`CREDITS-NEEDED.md` for anything without a photographer.

Film: `tools/import_film.py` → mp4 + webm, silent, looping, poster frame, ≤4 MB.

## Language

The words **luxury, world-class, opulent/opulence, high-end** do not appear on the
site. The brief was opulence *without* the words, and the client's own messaging
document never uses one of them. `check_site.py` rule 4 enforces this.

*Elegance* is permitted — the PDF uses it deliberately in **Where It Began** to argue
that elegance is not what makes a gathering matter. *Bespoke* is permitted; the PDF
uses it in Signature Moments.

Proof, not adjectives: the credentials strip, the five-movement process, the
role-attributed testimonials, and the Perspectives journal exist because a risk-averse
buyer discounts assertion and reads evidence.
